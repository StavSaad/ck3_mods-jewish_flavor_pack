# Rising Lion v0.16.0 — The Exilarchs and the Maccabees

Started: 2026-10-24 (split from the 0.15 plan)  
Status: planning; built early, in 0.15: Pesach (section 1), Kohanism's lock (section 6) and the Temple's
two great projects (section 7). Plan expanded 2026-11-13: build order, the feasts' chains, the Geonim, the
open questions' recommended answers, sections 11-13

The Jewish side of the Aramaic world: the festivals of the Jewish year, the Exilarchs of both lines and the
house they displaced, the lost heirs of the Maccabees, and Eldad ha-Dani.

## Build order

Each step is playable on its own and the later ones lean on the earlier.

1. **The feasts** (section 2): Purim, Sukkot, Hanukkah. All the machinery exists; only variants, events, art
2. **The feud and the Geonim** (section 4): the house relation at game start, the two Gaon positions, Saadia.
   Small, and every Exilarch story needs them
3. **The Rabbinic Exilarch story** (section 4), then the Karaite one from its shared effects
4. **The Hasmonean heir** (section 3): history, the bridge generator, the house; then its story (section 4)
5. **Beit Nathan's story** (section 4): needs the Rabbinic story's crises as its openings
6. **The Palestinian Gaonate** (section 8): needs Sukkot and the Rabbinic story
7. **Eldad ha-Dani** (section 5) and **Helena of Adiabene** (section 10): independent, any time
8. **Reconciliation** (section 4): last; needs both Exilarch stories and in-game tests of `set_parent_faith`
9. **The Nagid** (section 9) matters only from 1066 or late in an 867 game: last, or 0.17

Sections 11-13 are candidates, not yet decided.

**Depends on 0.15** ([v0.15_children_of_aram.md](v0.15_children_of_aram.md)):
- the generated feast override (`make_feast_override.py`), its visibility rule and the opening of feasts to
  Mishpacha heads (0.15 section 9)
- the story-cycle pattern of the Sabian storyline (0.15 section 10)

## 2. More Jewish feasts

Through the same override, each with its own cooldown variable and event chain, and each hidden unless the
host has the faith and it's the season. Months are approximate windows for the lunar calendar.

| Variant | Faiths (`is_shown`) | Months | Core |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purim** `jip_feast_type_purim` | Rabbinic and Karaite (Karaites read Esther too); not Haymanot or Samaritans, who don't keep it | 2–3 | Megillah reading; drinking "until one can't tell Haman from Mordecai" (the wine option); gifts of food and alms to the poor; held at Hamadan, the tomb of Esther and Mordecai, for more |
| **Sukkot** `jip_feast_type_sukkot` | All Jewish faiths (biblical) | 9–10 | The booths and the four species; harvest; Simchat Torah at the end (a Babylonian custom); a pilgrimage feast once the Temple stands |
| **Hanukkah** `jip_feast_type_hanukkah` | Rabbinic only (Karaites, Haymanot and Samaritans don't keep it) | 11–12 | The lights; the Maccabean victory: prestige and martial flavour; legitimacy for members of the Hasmonean heirs' house (section 3) |

Each needs what Pesach has: a `VARIANTS` and `BACKGROUNDS` entry in `make_feast_override.py`, a
`jip_is_<variant>` trigger, a branch in `jip_feast_meal_phase_effect` and `jip_feast_toast_phase_effect`, a
cooldown variable, and planning-window art by `make_feast_type_art.py`. Events in `jip_feast_events.txt`:
Purim 7000s, Sukkot 8000s, Hanukkah 9000s (1000-6000 are taken).

### Purim (7000s)
- [ ] 7001 **The Megillah**: the scroll read aloud, the hall drowning out Haman's name. A host with a
  rival or a hostile liege may name their own Haman under their breath (stress loss; a small chance a guest
  carries the word)
- [ ] 7002 **Mishloach manot and gifts to the poor**: send portions to guests (opinion) and alms (piety, the
  capital's or estate's county opinion; more with a Gabbai Tzedakah)
- [ ] 7003 **Ad de-lo yada**: with the feast's normal or extensive wine, drink until Haman and Mordecai
  blur: stress loss, a chance of `drunkard`, a chance of a loose-tongued guest's secret. Temperate hosts
  may keep sober (piety)
- [ ] 7004 **The Purim play**: a masked jester mocks the powerful; laugh (opinion of the guests), or take
  offence. From the 12th century only (the Purim-shpil is late); earlier, a paytan's parody
- [ ] 7005 summary: *"The Jews had light and gladness"* (Esther 8:16), `jip_purim_gladness` for a year
- [ ] **Faiths**: Karaites read the scroll but hold no rabbinic banquet rules and fix the day by their own
  moon (a line of text, as Pesach's 5006)
- [ ] **Hamadan** (`c_hamadan`): the tomb of Esther and Mordecai; the tradition is first told by Benjamin
  of Tudela (12th century), so the bonus is piety "as the Jews of Hamadan say", at any date
- [ ] **A second Purim** (candidate): communities kept their own Purims for a deliverance (Narbonne 1236,
  Cairo 1524). When Shtadlanut lifts an expulsion or wins a Letter of Protection under a hostile lord, or
  the family rides out a blood libel, the head may found one: a house modifier and a yearly household
  event on its day

### Sukkot (8000s)
- [ ] 8001 **The booth**: raised in the court or on the estate's roof; the guests are named (the
  *ushpizin* are Zoharic, 13th century: before 1280 simply honoured guests)
- [ ] 8002 **The four species**: the etrog is the hard one. Far from the Mediterranean (outside the mod's
  citron lands: the Levant, Egypt, the Maghreb, Iberia, Italy, Greece) it must be bought from a Radhanite or
  a caravan: gold for a fine one, or make do (less piety). A Mishpacha head with a Shaliach gets it free
- [ ] 8003 **The water-drawing** (Simchat Beit ha-Shoeva): torches and dancing, "who has not seen it has not
  seen joy" (Sukkah 5:1); prowess or diplomacy flavour
- [ ] 8004 **Hoshana Rabba and Simchat Torah**: the scroll finished and begun again; the host may give the
  last reading to a guest (opinion) or take it (piety). Simchat Torah is the Babylonian one-year cycle's
  feast; a host in the Land of Israel before about 1200 keeps the three-year cycle and has none (text only)
- [ ] 8005 summary; with the Temple standing, the pilgrimage to Jerusalem and the seventy bulls for the
  nations (piety, and opinion of non-Jewish neighbours)
- [ ] **Faiths**: Karaites build the booth out of the four species (Nehemiah 8:15) and wave nothing;
  Samaritans hang theirs with fruit indoors; Beta Israel keep Ba'ala Matsallat without a booth
- [ ] **The Mount of Olives assembly** (section 8) hangs on 8004 for a Palestinian Gaon in Jerusalem

### Hanukkah (9000s)
- [ ] 9001 **The lights**: the lamp at the door or, "in time of danger", on the table within (Shabbat 21b):
  a host under a hostile liege chooses (piety against risk)
- [ ] 9002 **The Scroll of Antiochus**: medieval communities read Megillat Antiochus (Saadia put it into
  Arabic); the tale of Judah and his brothers: martial flavour, prestige, a chance of martial lifestyle XP;
  after about 950 the *Josippon* is read beside it (section 3)
- [ ] 9003 **Judith's feast**: cheese and wine for Judith, whom the Middle Ages tied to Hanukkah; honours
  the women of the court (spouse and daughters' opinion)
- [ ] 9004 summary: `jip_hanukkah_lights`; legitimacy for the Hasmonean heirs' house; held in Jerusalem
  with the Temple standing it is the Rededication itself (the Hasmonean story's last act, section 4)
- [ ] No dreidels or gelt (early modern)

### The year outside the feast hall
Like the household seder (5020): one small event a year for Jewish playable characters who held no grand
feast, rotating so no one gets four a year.
- [ ] `jip_household_feast_on_action` grows from the seder's: each year one of Pesach, Sukkot, Hanukkah or
  Purim (whichever the character's faith keeps and hasn't seen longest)
- [ ] **Yom Kippur** (candidate, not a feast): a yearly choice for a stressed or sinful Jewish ruler: ask a
  rival's forgiveness (ends a rivalry at a prestige cost, if the rival agrees) or fast through it (piety,
  stress). Every Jewish faith; the Samaritans and Karaites on their own day
- [ ] **Tisha be-Av** (candidate): mourning for the Temple; no event once the Temple stands, except the
  first year: the fast turned to a feast (Zechariah 8:19)

## 3. A Hasmonean heir

A medieval character descended from the Maccabees, for Hanukkah's legitimacy bonus (section 2) and as a
claimant to the priest-kingship.

**Where the record ends**
- `history/characters/jip_hasmonean.txt` runs from Yochanan the Kohen (-243) to the Hasmonean–Herodian heirs
  of Herod and Mariamne: Agrippa II, Berenice, Drusilla (d. 79), Mariamne VI (d. 93)
- Hasmonean blood survives only through Mariamne's sons. Agrippa I's line dies out around 100 CE
- The longest line is **Alexander's** (`alexander_yanai_iii`, d. -7), whose children aren't in the mod yet:
  Tigranes V and Alexander → Tigranes VI of Armenia → Gaius Julius Alexander, king in Cilicia → Gaius
  Julius Alexander Berenicianus, Roman consul in 116 CE. Josephus says this branch gave up Jewish ways
  for Greek ones (*Antiquities* 18.139–141)
- Nothing documents any descent after the 2nd century, so any 9th-century heir needs invented generations

**Approach (decided): the documented line plus a generated bridge, nothing else.** No Hasmonean trait and
no claimed-descent mechanic; descent is the genealogy itself.
- [ ] Add Alexander's branch to `jip_hasmonean.txt` down to Berenicianus (116 CE). The people (Josephus,
  *Antiquities* 18.139-141; Kokkinos; check each date):
  - Alexander (`alexander_yanai_iii`, d. -7) and Glaphyra of Cappadocia → Tigranes V, king of Armenia
    (childless), and Alexander
  - Alexander → Tigranes VI, king of Armenia under Nero
  - Tigranes VI → Gaius Julius Alexander, given a kingdom in Cilicia (Cetis) by Vespasian, married Iotapa of
    Commagene
  - their children: Gaius Julius Agrippa (quaestor of Asia), Gaius Julius Alexander Berenicianus (consul
    116) and Julia Iotapa
  - the faith turns at Tigranes V and his brother's children: Josephus says they "deserted the Jewish
    religion" from birth; in the mod Hellenic (vanilla's `hellenic_pagan`), culture Greek, then Christian in
    the bridge from the 4th century
- [ ] `make_hasmonean_line.py` generates the generations from 116 to 867 (about 25) in a new history file:
  names from the right name lists (`name_list_hebrew` while Judean, Byzantine Greek ones after the drift), plausible dates, one line of heirs with a few siblings
- [ ] The twist Josephus gives us: the line drifts into the Greek world, so the 867 heir is a Byzantine
  Greek noble family that remembers a Maccabean ancestor. A decision lets them **return to the faith of the
  Maccabees** (convert to Judaism and take up the heritage), with the fallout conversion brings in Byzantium
- [ ] **Recognising descent without a trait** (decided): the bridge is its **own house within
  `herodian_dynasty`** (Alexander's dynasty in the mod), defined in the mod's dynasty houses, and Hasmonean
  content checks membership of it (e.g. `house = house:house_berenikianos`, keyed like the mod's
  `house_ananite` in `common/dynasty_houses/jip_houses.txt`). The house starts with Alexander's line;
  earlier Herodians stay in the main house

**Gameplay once there's an heir**
- [ ] **Restore the Hasmonean priest-kingship**: a Hasmonean heir of a Jewish faith who holds Jerusalem (and
  the Temple, once rebuilt) can hold the Kohen Gadol title (`d_kohen_gadol`) together with the crown, as the
  Hasmoneans did
- [ ] Hanukkah legitimacy for members of the heirs' house (section 2)
- [ ] Josippon: the 10th-century Hebrew history (southern Italy) that made the Maccabees famous again; an
  event when it appears brings the house prestige

**Open questions, with a recommended answer (to confirm)**
- **Culture and faith at 867**: Greek and Orthodox. A bridge that had already returned to Judaism would
  throw away the story's first three acts
- **Home**: Ancyra (`b_ankyra` in `c_galatia`). The one trace of the family after Berenicianus is there: an
  inscription of Gaius Julius Severus of Ancyra (consul about 138) calls him kin of "King Alexander" among
  other consulars (OGIS 544; check). Second choice: Seleucia in Cilicia, by King Alexander's old kingdom
- **Standing**: a Byzantine noble family, not a ruler: with Roads to Power a landless administrative house
  with an estate in the Bucellarian theme (playable from the map, like Thabit); without it a courtier of
  the theme's strategos, unplayable until landed. A count would be simpler but Ancyra's holder at 867 is
  the theme's governor, and unseating him for an invented family is worse history
- **The house's name**: **Berenikianos** (Βερενικιανός), the consul's own cognomen worn down into a
  Byzantine family name: real, Greek, and its Jewish root (Berenice, the Herodian queen) hidden in plain
  sight. The heir: **Alexandros Berenikianos**, the name the line repeats every other generation
- **The memory** (the story's first act): a signet with a palm branch and a Hebrew legend no one can read,
  kept since the kings of Cilicia

**The bridge's shape** (for `make_hasmonean_line.py`)
- 116-300: Roman senators and Galatian notables, Hellenic, names from the family's stock (Alexandros,
  Tigranes, Agrippas, Berenikianos, Iotape, Berenike, Mariamme for one daughter a century)
- 300-600: Christian from Constantine's century; curials and bishops' kin of Ancyra
- 600-867: the theme's soldiers, through the Persian and Arab sacks of Ancyra (622, 838): one generation
  flees and returns, a place for the memory nearly to be lost
- seeded, so reruns give the same line; one heir per generation, born when the father is 25-35, with one to
  three siblings who die childless

## 4. Story cycles: the Hasmoneans and the Exilarchs

Long stories carried across generations, like the 0.15 Sabian storyline and vanilla's
`ep3_story_cycle_restoring_rome` and `ep3_story_cycle_hasan_sabbah`.

### Shared structure
- [ ] One story type per line, in `common/story_cycles/jip_*_story_cycle.txt`
- [ ] **Owner**: a character; when the owner dies the story passes on: to the heir for a house story, to the
  new title holder for a title story
- [ ] Effect groups on yearly or longer intervals fire the stage's events; the stage is a story variable
- [ ] Visible decisions for the player's own milestones; the story paces events and crises around them
- [ ] Shared building blocks (installation, crisis, caliph's favour) as scripted effects, so the two
  Exilarch stories don't duplicate each other
- [ ] **What 0.15 already gives**: the story window (`gui/window_situation_list.gui`, generated by
  `make_story_window_override.py`) shows an act name, a 0-10 counter, a patron and a list of people. Each new
  story gets its act custom loc and reuses the counter the way the Sabians' standing does, so no new GUI
  beyond a block per story in the generator
- [ ] **AI owners**: the Sabian story is the player's; these four also run for AI owners, quietly (no
  events shown, outcomes rolled), so a player Exilarch meets a living Beit Nathan and the reverse
- [ ] **One counter each** (0-10, changed only through raise/lower effects, as `jip_sabian_standing`):
  - Rabbinic: **the Caliph's favour** (at 0 disfavour and exile; at 10 the reign's privileges)
  - Karaite: **the Mourners of Zion** (the community gathered in Jerusalem)
  - Beit Nathan: **the claim's strength** (elders and Geonim who uphold it)
  - Hasmonean: **the memory** (0 ends the story in Act I; 10 opens the return)

### The Geonim (shared by every Exilarch story)
The stories need the heads of the two Babylonian academies as people, to quarrel with, appoint and be banned
by.
- [ ] **Two court positions of the Rabbinic Exilarch**, **Gaon of Sura** and **Gaon of Pumbedita**
  (`jip_gaon_sura_court_position`, `jip_gaon_pumbedita_court_position`), not titles: the Exilarch did
  confirm the Geonim, and a position gives a character, an opinion, a salary and a dismissal for free.
  Holder a learned adult Rabbinic Jew (learning 14+), for life unless deposed; dismissing one is a crisis,
  not a click (the ban events)
- [ ] The holder of `d_exilarchate` only (`jip_is_exilarch_trigger` and the Rabbinic line); the Rosh
  Yeshiva stays the family's own teacher
- [ ] Filled at game start for Hasdai II from generated scholars (the Geonim of 867: Nahshon bar Zadok at
  Sura from about 871, Tzemah bar Paltoi at Pumbedita 872; before them the lists are thin, so generated
  names are honest for 867) and refilled by event when one dies: the academy sends two candidates
- [ ] **Each Gaon has a temper** (the character's own traits): an ambitious or arrogant Gaon under a weak
  Exilarch (low legitimacy, low Caliph's favour) starts the quarrel
- [ ] **Saadia** (882-942): born in the Fayyum by history file (`c_fayyum`), a Mishpacha courtier in Egypt;
  at about 33 he travels to Tiberias and then Babylon (event for the Exilarch about 915-928: "a scholar
  from Egypt", offered as Gaon of Sura, as David ben Zakkai offered it in 928 against Nissim Naharwani's
  warning). Learning 25+, `stubborn`, `zealous`. With him as Gaon the quarrel and the Karaite polemics get
  their sharpest forms; if he dies young or the player passes him over, a generated Gaon plays the part
  more mildly

### `story_jip_hasmonean_return`: the return of the Maccabees
Owner: head of the Hasmonean heirs' house (section 3), from 867.
1. **Memory**: a family tradition in Byzantium (an old seal, an ancestor's name, a Hebrew prayer said
   without understanding); each generation it may grow or fade, and if it fades the story ends
2. **Seeking**: a Jewish teacher, secret study; from the mid-10th century the *Josippon* retells the
   Maccabees' story
3. **The return to the faith**: the decision from section 3. Byzantium is dangerous for it: Basil I ordered
   the Jews baptised in 873. Roads out: flight to **Khazaria** (the mod's Khazar start; marriage into the
   Khazar house), to the caliphate, or to southern Italy
4. **The Land**: settle in the Land of Israel; win or be granted Jerusalem
5. **Rededication**: the Temple (the mod's Temple in Jerusalem building), Hanukkah held in Jerusalem, and
   the Hasmonean priest-kingship (crown plus `d_kohen_gadol`, section 3)

### `story_jip_exilarchate_rabbinic`: the Rabbinic Exilarch
Owner: holder of `d_exilarchate` (Hasdai II of the Bostanites at 867).
- **Installation**: on each new Exilarch, the ceremony described by Nathan ha-Bavli (10th century): the
  Geonim and elders gather, homage on the Sabbath, the procession, the audience with the caliph. Outcomes
  set the reign's standing
- **The Exilarch's court**: yearly dues from the communities, appointing judges, the caliph's goodwill (or
  his vizier's fines)
- **Exilarch against Gaon**: the great quarrels with the academies of Sura and Pumbedita, after David ben
  Zakkai and Saadia Gaon (930s): bans, a rival Exilarch raised by the other side, the caliph's arbitration
- **Disfavour and exile**: an Exilarch banished by the caliph, as Mar Ukba was to Kairouan
- **Decline**: the office weakens after 1040 (the last Exilarch, Hezekiah, imprisoned) and revives under
  the Abbasids in the 12th century (Benjamin of Tudela's account), so later starts get different stages

Unlike the Sabian story this one has no last act: it is a reign's story, restarted at each installation.
- [ ] **Each reign**: Installation → the Court (the long middle; favour moves) → at most one great crisis
  (the quarrel, or disfavour) → the reign's verdict at death (a nickname and a house modifier for the next
  installation: "son of a beloved Exilarch" or "of a deposed one")
- [ ] **Installation** (three events): the Geonim's consent (each Gaon's opinion; a hostile one demands a
  price), the Sabbath homage (the sermon: learning or diplomacy), the audience (a gift to the caliph sets
  the starting favour; the vizier may want his own). An Exilarch who isn't the Caliph's subject (the
  estate moved, the caliphate gone) installs before whoever rules Baghdad, or with no patron and no favour
  counter at all
- [ ] **The Court's decisions** (story group, like the Sabians'): Collect the Communities' Dues (yearly
  gold against the families' opinion; the reshuyot: which lands pay the Exilarch and which the academies),
  Appoint a Judge (to a Jewish county or a family's Beit Din), Seek an Audience (favour, for gold or a
  hook)
- [ ] **Exilarch against Gaon**, a chain with a rival: the Gaon refuses to sign a judgment (David ben
  Zakkai's inheritance case) → bans fly both ways → the Gaon names a counter-Exilarch from the house (a
  brother or cousin: a claimant with a claim on `d_exilarchate`, feeding the elective) while the Exilarch
  names a counter-Gaon → the caliph arbitrates for whoever has more favour, gold or friends at court →
  years later, a reconciliation on a fast day (historically 937, at Purim: tie to section 2)
- [ ] **Disfavour** at favour 0: fined, then banished: the estate moves to Kairouan (`c_kairwan`, as Mar
  Ukba's did about 913) or wherever the player chooses outside the caliphate; the title stays, a regent
  cousin sits in Baghdad, and the road back is favour regained from abroad or a new caliph
- [ ] **Player as Gaon's side**: none; the Geonim aren't playable. A Mishpacha head in the caliphate is
  asked to back one side in the quarrel (opinion, a favour owed)

### `story_jip_exilarchate_karaite`: the Karaite Nasi
Owner: holder of `d_karaite_exilarchate` (Boaz I of the Ananites at 867).
- **The Mourners of Zion**: Daniel al-Qumisi (9th century) calls Karaites to Jerusalem; the Nasi can
  move the community's centre there: ascetics, mourning for Zion, the Karaite golden age of Jerusalem
- **Polemics**: Saadia Gaon's attacks and the Karaite replies (Salmon ben Jeroham), as debate events with
  the Rabbinic Exilarch and the Geonim
- **The calendar**: sighting the new moon and the ripening barley (*aviv*) in the Land, a yearly event that
  ties the Karaites to Jerusalem
- **Missions**: Karaite scholars and merchants to Byzantium and Egypt
- **The end in Jerusalem**: the crusader conquest of 1099 (Karaite books and captives ransomed); later
  centres in Egypt and Constantinople

### `story_jip_beit_nathan`: the lost crown
Owner: head of Beit Nathan (`nathanite_dynasty`; Pinchas ben Abdimi's Mishpacha family at 867), the house of
the Exilarchs before Bostanai.
- **The old claim**: Beit Nathan descends from the Exilarchs of the Talmud and Persian era (Huna, Zutra,
  Kahana); the story keeps the claim alive with genealogies, elders and Geonim who remember
- **The Bostanai controversy**: Bostanai's sons by his Jewish wife called the sons of the Persian princess
  slaves, and their legitimacy was disputed for centuries. Beit Nathan can use it against whichever
  Bostanite holds the title
- **The road back**: the vanilla Claim Exilarchate decision and scheme (already in the mod), alliances with
  a Gaon in a quarrel (the Rabbinic story's crises are openings), courting electors
- **Another crown**: if the Exilarchate stays out of reach, found a Davidic *nesiut* elsewhere, as Davidic
  families later did in Mosul, Damascus and Egypt
- **Ending**: Beit Nathan regains `d_exilarchate`, founds its own nesiut, or the line dies out

### The feud of Beit Bustenai and Beit Anan
The two Exilarchic houses come from one family. In the Rabbanite telling, Anan ben David was passed over
for the Exilarchate in favour of his younger brother Hananiah (c. 760s), was imprisoned by the caliph, and
founded the sect that became the Karaites. The Bostanite and Ananite lines have been rivals ever since.

- [ ] **A vanilla house relation at feud level** between the Bostanite house and the Ananite house, set at
  game start with `set_house_relation` (`level = feud`, via vanilla's `set_house_relation_level_effect`),
  wherever both houses exist. Both heads start as playable Abbasid vassals, so vanilla's
  `default_house_relation` accepts it (shared top liege)
- [ ] **What the feud level gives** (vanilla): -30 opinion between members, hostile scheme bonus, eradicate
  casus belli, cheaper casus belli prestige, fewer marriages, fewer guests at each other's activities, more
  likely war targets
- [ ] **Reason text**: a description key like `house_relation_reason_jip_anan_passed_over_desc` ("Anan was
  passed over for his brother")
- [ ] **Moving the relation**: the story cycles shift it with `change_house_relation_level`
  - deeper: polemics between the two Exilarchs (the Karaite story's Saadia events), a rival Exilarch raised
    by either side, a slight at a feast
  - easing: a marriage between the houses, standing together against a caliph's fines or a persecution, a
    shared Pesach (section 1)
  - full reconciliation only as a rare, hard-won outcome; the schism stays
- [ ] **Starting heads at 867**: Hasdai II (Bostanite, Rabbinic Exilarch) and Boaz I (Ananite, Karaite Nasi)
- [x] **The houses' keys** (checked): the Ananites are already a cadet house of the Bostanite dynasty,
  `house_ananite` (`dynasty = bostanite_dynasty`, `common/dynasty_houses/jip_houses.txt`), so the feud is
  inside one dynasty, as it was in history. The Bostanite main house has no key of its own (the engine
  makes it from the dynasty): reach it through its people, `character:<Hasdai's id>.house`, saved as a
  scope at game start. A feud between two houses of one dynasty also means the dynast (whoever has more
  renown standing) is one side's head: test that vanilla's house relations accept a same-dynasty pair, and
  that the dynast's powers (disinherit, denounce) don't make the feud one-sided; if they do, close those
  interactions between the two houses

### Reconciliation: one Israel again
The way out of the feud: Rabbanites and Karaites rejoin, either as rites of one faith or by the smaller
community merging into the dominant one. History never got that far, but it came close to coexistence: in
Fatimid Egypt the two communities cooperated, intermarried with marriage contracts that honoured both
customs, and shared leaders (Rustow). That's the ground this path builds on.

**Where things stand in the mod**
- `rabbinic_faith` has the rites `rabbinism` and `merkabah`; `karaism` is its own faith with one rite,
  `karaism` (`common/religion/rite_types/jip_jewish_rite_types.txt`), both in `judaism_religion`
- **Engine effects** (from the docs in `ck3.exe`):
  - `set_parent_faith = { target = <faith> main = no include_derived = yes }` on a rite moves it into
    another faith
  - `set_character_rite` and `set_county_rite` convert characters and counties to a rite
  - `detach_rite_to_new_faith` splits a rite off into a new faith (for a renewed schism)
  - `set_can_convert_to_rite` opens or closes a rite to conversion

**The road there**
- [ ] **Easing the feud first**: the house relation must have climbed out of feud (cordial or better) through
  the story cycles' events: marriages, joint stands against the caliph, shared feasts
- [ ] **Mixed marriages**: a marriage between members of the two communities can carry a double contract
  (both customs honoured), easing the relation and making the next step likelier (the Geniza contracts)
- [ ] **The assembly**: a decision for either Exilarch, once relations allow, to call the other, the Geonim
  and the Karaite scholars to an assembly. An event chain of debate (learning, diplomacy, piety, the
  attendees' opinions and zeal) decides the outcome. AI Exilarchs need high opinion and low zeal to agree

**Outcomes**
1. **Union of rites** (the two are close in strength, or the assembly agrees on equal standing)
   - the smaller faith's rite moves into the larger with `set_parent_faith` (usually Karaite into
     `rabbinic_faith`, giving it Rabbinic, Merkabah and Karaite rites); if Karaism is the larger, the
     Rabbinic rites move instead
   - characters and counties keep their rite and now share one faith: no faith hostility between them
   - both titles survive: `d_karaite_exilarchate` becomes the Karaite rite's Nasi alongside the Exilarch
   - test in game: what the emptied faith does, and whether the engine swaps the moved rite's tenets or
     doctrines (its docs mention divergence-driven swaps when rites change)
2. **Merge into the dominant faith** (one side clearly stronger: e.g. far more counties and followers, the
   holy sites, or it won the assembly or a war under the feud's eradicate casus belli)
   - the minority converts to the dominant faith's main rite: counties over time (a fast conversion
     modifier, then `set_county_rite`), characters by choice, with zealots refusing (they keep the old faith,
     or hold it in secret)
   - the losing line's Exilarch title becomes ceremonial or is destroyed
3. **Failure**: the assembly breaks up; the relation drops back towards feud and a cooldown starts

**Afterwards**
- [ ] **A renewed schism**: in a union, if the houses fall back into feud and zeal runs high, the Karaite (or
  Rabbinic) rite can split off again through `detach_rite_to_new_faith`
- [ ] **Measuring dominance**: a script value comparing the two faiths' counties, followers and holy sites,
  shown in the decision's tooltip so the player knows which outcome is in reach
- [ ] **Who it's for**: available only while both houses (and both Exilarchates) exist; the Hasmonean and
  Beit Nathan stories react to it (a reunited Israel raises the Hasmonean return's prestige)

**Reading**: Marina Rustow, *Heresy and the Politics of Community: The Jews of the Fatimid Caliphate*
(Cornell, 2008)

## 7. The Temple as a great project

*(The next two parts are the design as written before the build; "The projects" below is what stands.)*

Before 0.15 the Temple was one decision, `clear_grounds_for_the_third_temple_decision`
(`common/decisions/jip_jewish_decisions.txt`): an independent Jewish ruler of Israelite heritage who holds
Jerusalem and completely controls `k_israel`, at piety level 4, pays 500 piety and fires `jip_hebrew.0041`,
whose `rebuild_the_third_temple_effect` removes the Dome of the Rock, places `temple_in_jerusalem_01`, takes
Jerusalem from the Islamic faiths' holy sites and costs them 15 fervour. Turn this into vanilla great
projects, so the Temple is built over years with others' help, and its cost is felt while it rises.

### How vanilla does it (the model)
- Great projects live in `common/great_projects/types/` (documented in `_great_project_types.info`):
  `is_shown`, `can_start_planning`, `is_location_valid`, `province_filter`, `owner`, `cost`,

  "same faith" option
- **DLC**: the cathedral is gated by `has_pam_dlc_trigger` and the Chinese projects by
  `has_tgp_dlc_trigger`. Check whether the great-project interface works without either; if not, keep the
  decision as the fallback for players without them

## 8. The Palestinian Gaonate

The yeshiva of the Land of Israel, at Tiberias and from the early 10th century in Jerusalem (after 1071 Tyre,
then Damascus). Its geonim came from a few priestly families (Ben Meir; the Kohen family of Evyatar
ha-Kohen), and once from a Davidic one: Daniel ben Azarya, from Babylon, held it 1051–1062.

- [ ] **Title** `d_palestinian_gaonate`, landless and built like `d_exilarchate`; seat Tiberias (`c_tiberias`)
  at 867, moving to Jerusalem by an event in the early 10th century
- [ ] **Holder at 867**: a new Mishpacha family (the Ben Meir house); succession elective among the yeshiva's
  priestly families
- [ ] **The Mount of Olives assembly**: on Hoshana Rabba, the end of Sukkot (section 2), a Gaon who holds
  Sukkot in Jerusalem holds the assembly: proclaims the year's calendar, gathers pilgrims and donations,
  and may ban the Karaites (Karaite opinion falls; the Bostanai–Anan feud, section 4, deepens)
- [ ] **Against Babylon**: the Ben Meir calendar dispute (921–922), when the Palestinian Gaon proclaimed a
  calendar Saadia and the Babylonian academies rejected: a crisis in the Rabbinic Exilarch story where the
  Exilarch must choose a side, and the losing side loses standing
- [ ] **A Davidic Gaon**: after Daniel ben Azarya, the Rabbinic and Beit Nathan stories can put a Davidic
  candidate forward
- [ ] **Correction to check**: the Ben Meir family claimed descent from Rabbi Judah the Patriarch (Hillel's
  house), not from priests; the priestly geonim are the later house of Solomon ha-Kohen (11th century, down
  to Evyatar). So 867's holder is a Ben Meir of the Patriarch's line (a generated ancestor of Aaron ben
  Meir, the Gaon of 921; no Gaon of the 860s is known by name), and the electors are the yeshiva's seven
  (the *haverim*), not priestly families
- [ ] **Succession**: its own elective (a copy of the Exilarchate's): candidates the holder's house and the
  Av Beit Din (the yeshiva's second, who by custom moved up), electors the Mishpacha heads of the Levant
  and Egypt
- [ ] **Title and rank**: Gaon, "Head of the Yeshiva of the Pride of Jacob" (*Rosh Yeshivat Geon
  Yaakov*); flavorization priority with the Exilarch's (310). A Familial head with an estate, so all the
  family mechanics work; the Yeshiva estate building at its top level from the start
- [ ] **What the office does**
  - **Proclaims the calendar** on the Mount of Olives each Sukkot: piety, and standing with every Rabbinic
    family west of the Euphrates
  - **The communities of the West**: dues from Egypt, the Levant and (loosely) Italy, against the
    Exilarch's from Babylon and the east: the two offices' rival decisions pull the same families
  - **The Egyptian purse**: the Gaonate lived on Fustat's gifts and the Fatimid court's grant; a Gaon needs
    a friend in Egypt (the Nagid, section 9, once there is one)
- [ ] **The moves**: Tiberias → Jerusalem (event, 900-950, or at once if a Jewish ruler holds Jerusalem) →
  Tyre if Jerusalem falls to Turks or Franks (the estate moves; 1071) → Damascus. Each is the family
  estate's ordinary move, announced by an event
- [ ] **For a Jewish king of Israel**: the Gaon is his realm's sage; the Gaon may crown him (legitimacy), and
  the Sanhedrin question waits (section 13)

## 9. The Nagid of Egypt

The head of the Jews (*ra'is al-yahud*) of Fatimid Egypt, appointed by the caliph over Rabbanites, Karaites
and Samaritans alike. The office appears around 1065 (Judah ibn Saadia, then his brother Mevorakh); from
1082 to 1094 David ben Daniel, Daniel ben Azarya's Davidic son, held it.

- [ ] **Title** `d_negidut_egypt`, landless, seat `c_cairo`
- [ ] **Creation**: an interaction or decision by the ruler of Egypt to appoint a Jewish notable at court or a
  Mishpacha head in Egypt, once Egypt is independent of Baghdad (the Tulunids from 868, the Fatimids from
  969); in history at the 1066 start
- [ ] **Holder at 1066**: Judah ibn Saadia (check dates: Cohen and Rustow differ on when the office began)
- [ ] **Powers**: appoints judges and collects communal dues in Egypt; a bonus to Shtadlanut with the ruler of
  Egypt; presides over every Jewish faith there, so the Nagid helps the reconciliation path (section 4)
- [ ] **Beit Nathan's "another crown"** (section 4): the Nagid's office is a target for Davidic houses, after
  David ben Daniel
- [ ] **Disfavour**: the ruler of Egypt can dismiss the Nagid, like the caliph's crises in the Exilarch story
- [ ] **Dates to use** (Cohen; check): Judah ben Saadia about 1064-1078, his brother Mevorakh 1078-1082 and
  again 1094-1111, David ben Daniel between them. Both brothers were court physicians, which is how the
  office was won
- [ ] **How it is won in play**: not by decision alone. A Jewish physician, Court Financier or Mishpacha
  head whom the ruler of Egypt likes (opinion, a hook, years of service) is offered the headship by event;
  a player family in Egypt can ask for it through a fifth Shtadlanut request (`actor` in
  `make_shtadlan_interactions.py`), hard unless they serve at court
- [ ] **Other Negidim** (candidate): the same office under other crowns, one title each, created the same
  way: Kairouan (Abraham ben Nathan, about 1015), al-Andalus (Hasdai ibn Shaprut at Cordoba, about 950;
  Samuel ha-Nagid at Granada, 1027, vizier and general), Yemen. Samuel ha-Nagid is the one worth a
  character: a Jewish vizier who led a Muslim king's armies and wrote war poetry
- [ ] **No history before 1065**: at 867 the title doesn't exist; no invented Negidim under the Tulunids

## 10. Queen Helena of Adiabene

Helena, queen of Adiabene (`d_kurdistan`, Hadyab in the Aramaic lists; capital Arbela), and her son King Izates
converted to Judaism in the 1st century (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.17-96); she built a palace and her tomb in
Jerusalem and fed the city in a famine. Moved here from the 0.15 plan (section 5), which gives Adiabene only its
name and CoA.

The design: three small pieces, each standing alone.

- [ ] **The Royal Converts** (event, once per ruler): a ruler of Arbela (`c_irbil`) or Adiabene
  (`d_kurdistan`) who isn't Jewish, of Syriac heritage (Aramean, Assyrian, Nabati), with a Jewish courtier,

  Ananias, who taught the king's wives and then the king. Options: hear more (learning; the event may

- [ ] **Monobaz's Treasure** (decision, a Jewish ruler of Adiabene, in a year a county of the realm or
  Jerusalem's has a famine or low control or development loss): open the treasury as Monobaz did ("my
  fathers stored below, I store above", Bava Batra 11a) and as Helena sent grain from Alexandria and figs
  from Cyprus: gold for piety, county opinion and, when the grain goes to Jerusalem, the opinion of

- [ ] **Helena's gifts to the Temple**: when Rebuild the Temple (section 7) is under way, a Jewish ruler of

## 11. The false messiah (candidate)

The age's recurring crisis, and the dark mirror of the Hasmonean and Davidic stories: Abu Isa of Isfahan
(8th century), Serene (about 720), David Alroy of Amadiya (about 1160, in Adiabene's mountains: Benjamin of
Tudela), the messiah Maimonides wrote of in the *Epistle to Yemen* (1172).

- [ ] **An event chain, not a mechanic**: every few decades, in a Jewish county or realm with Jewish
  families under a hostile or collapsing lord (war, high `jip_jewish_hostility_value`, a recent expulsion),
  a preacher rises. Generated character, high learning or diplomacy, `zealous`
- [ ] **For a Mishpacha head or Exilarch nearby**: back him (the community's fervour, gold from the
  believers; ruin if he fails), denounce him to the lord (the lord's favour, the families' anger: the
  Exilarch and the Gaon did write against Alroy), or wait
- [ ] **For the lord**: a revolt of the county (vanilla's peasant faction, Jewish), or the preacher's quiet
  end
- [ ] **For a Davidic or Hasmonean player**: the preacher may name *them*: legitimacy and danger at once
  (the liege's hostility); a Davidic king in Jerusalem with the Temple standing ends the chain for good
- [ ] **Maimonides' letter**: when the playable Maimonides (or any learning 20+ Rabbinic scholar) is
  asked, a letter of comfort instead of a ban: the families' stress and opinion

## 12. The Khazar letters (candidate)

The mod has a Khazar start and a Sephardi court; history joined them. Around 960 Hasdai ibn Shaprut, the
Cordoban caliph's Jewish physician and minister, wrote to King Joseph of the Khazars, and (in the texts we
have) got an answer telling of Bulan's conversion.

- [ ] **Letter events** (vanilla's letter window; no DLC banner there): a Jewish courtier, Court Financier or
  Nagid of a Muslim or Christian king learns that a Jewish king reigns on the steppe (any independent
  Jewish king of Khazar heritage) and writes. The Khazar ruler answers (prestige for both, an alliance
  offer, or a Radhanite caravan between them: a contract for Mishpacha families on the road)
- [ ] **The other way**: a Khazar player's letter to the Exilarch or the Gaon asks for teachers: a scholar
  for the court, and the choice of Rabbinic or Karaite teachers (the Karaites' own claim on Khazaria is
  late legend; keep it a rare option)
- [ ] **Eldad** (section 5) is cited in Hasdai's letter: if Eldad's journey ended well, the letter says so

## 13. Semikhah and the Sanhedrin (candidate; likely 0.17)

The capstone for a Jewish king in Jerusalem after the Temple: renewing ordination and the court of
seventy-one, as Maimonides ruled the sages of the Land could by agreement (*Hilkhot Sanhedrin* 4:11) and as
Jacob Berab tried at Safed in 1538.

- [ ] A decision for an independent Jewish ruler holding Jerusalem and Safed or Tiberias, with the
  Palestinian Gaon (section 8) in the realm and willing: the Gaon is ordained first, and the court sits
- [ ] The Exilarch and the Babylonian Geonim may refuse to recognise it (the Rabbinic story's last great
  quarrel); the Karaites certainly do
- [ ] What it gives is to design: a faith-wide head for Rabbinism (its doctrine has none), or a council of
  the realm. Too large for 0.16 unless the Gaonate lands early

## Decided

- Pesach, Purim, Sukkot and Hanukkah are feast variants through the 0.15 feast override, hidden unless the
  host has the faith and it's the season
- The Hasmonean heir comes only from the documented line plus a generated bridge, as its own house within
  `herodian_dynasty`; no trait, no claimed descent
- Eldad ha-Dani is a vanilla landless adventurer, Haymanot and Ethiopian
- No one can convert to Kohanism until the Temple in Jerusalem is rebuilt (and again if it's lost)
- Clearing the Temple Mount and rebuilding the Temple become two great projects instead of one decision
- Beit Bustenai and Beit Anan start in a feud through vanilla's default house relation (both are Abbasid
  vassals at the start, so it's valid)
