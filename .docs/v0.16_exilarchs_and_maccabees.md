# Rising Lion v0.16.0 — The Exilarchs and the Maccabees

Started: 2026-10-24 (split from the 0.15 plan)  
Status: planning; Pesach (section 1) built early, in 0.15

The Jewish side of the Aramaic world: the festivals of the Jewish year, the Exilarchs of both lines and the
house they displaced, the lost heirs of the Maccabees, and Eldad ha-Dani.

**Depends on 0.15** ([v0.15_beth_aramaye.md](v0.15_beth_aramaye.md)):
- the generated feast override (`make_feast_override.py`), its visibility rule and the opening of feasts to
  Mishpacha heads (0.15 section 9)
- the story-cycle pattern of the Sabian storyline (0.15 section 10)

## 3. A Hasmonean heir

A medieval character descended from the Maccabees, for Hanukkah's legitimacy bonus (section 2) and as a
claimant to the priest-kingship.

**Where the record ends**
- `history/characters/jip_hasmonean.txt` runs from Yochanan the Kohen (-243) to the Hasmonean–Herodian heirs
  of Herod and Mariamne: Agrippa II, Berenice, Drusilla (d. 79), Mariamne VI (d. 93)
- Hasmonean blood survives only through Mariamne's sons. Agrippa I's line dies out around 100 CE
- The longest line is **Alexander's** (`alexander_yanai_iii`, d. -7), whose children aren't in the mod yet:
  Tigranes V and Alexander → Tigranes VI of Armenia → Gaius Julius Alexander, king in Cilicia → Gaius
  Julius Alexander Berenicianus, Roman consul in 116 CE. Josephus says this branch gave up Jewish ways
  for Greek ones (*Antiquities* 18.139–141)
- Nothing documents any descent after the 2nd century, so any 9th-century heir needs invented generations

**Approach (decided): the documented line plus a generated bridge, nothing else.** No Hasmonean trait and
no claimed-descent mechanic; descent is the genealogy itself.
- [ ] Add Alexander's branch to `jip_hasmonean.txt` down to Berenicianus (116 CE)
- [ ] `make_hasmonean_line.py` generates the generations from 116 to 867 (about 25) in a new history file:
  names from the right name lists, plausible dates, one line of heirs with a few siblings
- [ ] The twist Josephus gives us: the line drifts into the Greek world, so the 867 heir is a Byzantine
  Greek noble family that remembers a Maccabean ancestor. A decision lets them **return to the faith of the
  Maccabees** (convert to Judaism and take up the heritage), with the fallout conversion brings in Byzantium
- [ ] **Recognising descent without a trait** (decided): the bridge is its **own house within
  `herodian_dynasty`** (Alexander's dynasty in the mod), defined in the mod's dynasty houses, and Hasmonean
  content checks membership of it (e.g. `house = house:jip_house_hasmonean_heirs`). The house starts with
  Alexander's line; earlier Herodians stay in the main house

**Gameplay once there's an heir**
- [ ] **Restore the Hasmonean priest-kingship**: a Hasmonean heir of a Jewish faith who holds Jerusalem (and
  the Temple, once rebuilt) can hold the Kohen Gadol title (`d_kohen_gadol`) together with the crown, as the
  Hasmoneans did
- [ ] Hanukkah legitimacy for members of the heirs' house (section 2)
- [ ] Josippon: the 10th-century Hebrew history (southern Italy) that made the Maccabees famous again; an
  event when it appears brings the house prestige

**Open questions**
- The heir's culture and faith at 867 (Byzantine Greek Christian, or a bridge that returned to Judaism
  earlier), where they live, and their name
- The house's name (Alexander's house? a Greek name the family carries in Byzantium?)

## 4. Story cycles: the Hasmoneans and the Exilarchs

Long stories carried across generations, like the 0.15 Sabian storyline and vanilla's
`ep3_story_cycle_restoring_rome` and `ep3_story_cycle_hasan_sabbah`.

### Shared structure
- [ ] One story type per line, in `common/story_cycles/jip_*_story_cycle.txt`
- [ ] **Owner**: a character; when the owner dies the story passes on: to the heir for a house story, to the
  new title holder for a title story
- [ ] Effect groups on yearly or longer intervals fire the stage's events; the stage is a story variable
- [ ] Visible decisions for the player's own milestones; the story paces events and crises around them
- [ ] Shared building blocks (installation, crisis, caliph's favour) as scripted effects, so the two
  Exilarch stories don't duplicate each other

### `story_jip_hasmonean_return`: the return of the Maccabees
Owner: head of the Hasmonean heirs' house (section 3), from 867.
1. **Memory**: a family tradition in Byzantium (an old seal, an ancestor's name, a Hebrew prayer said
   without understanding); each generation it may grow or fade, and if it fades the story ends
2. **Seeking**: a Jewish teacher, secret study; from the mid-10th century the *Josippon* retells the
   Maccabees' story
3. **The return to the faith**: the decision from section 3. Byzantium is dangerous for it: Basil I ordered
   the Jews baptised in 873. Roads out: flight to **Khazaria** (the mod's Khazar start; marriage into the
   Khazar house), to the caliphate, or to southern Italy
4. **The Land**: settle in the Land of Israel; win or be granted Jerusalem
5. **Rededication**: the Temple (the mod's Temple in Jerusalem building), Hanukkah held in Jerusalem, and
   the Hasmonean priest-kingship (crown plus `d_kohen_gadol`, section 3)

### `story_jip_exilarchate_rabbinic`: the Rabbinic Exilarch
Owner: holder of `d_exilarchate` (Hasdai II of the Bostanites at 867).
- **Installation**: on each new Exilarch, the ceremony described by Nathan ha-Bavli (10th century): the
  Geonim and elders gather, homage on the Sabbath, the procession, the audience with the caliph. Outcomes
  set the reign's standing
- **The Exilarch's court**: yearly dues from the communities, appointing judges, the caliph's goodwill (or
  his vizier's fines)
- **Exilarch against Gaon**: the great quarrels with the academies of Sura and Pumbedita, after David ben
  Zakkai and Saadia Gaon (930s): bans, a rival Exilarch raised by the other side, the caliph's arbitration
- **Disfavour and exile**: an Exilarch banished by the caliph, as Mar Ukba was to Kairouan
- **Decline**: the office weakens after 1040 (the last Exilarch, Hezekiah, imprisoned) and revives under
  the Abbasids in the 12th century (Benjamin of Tudela's account), so later starts get different stages

### `story_jip_exilarchate_karaite`: the Karaite Nasi
Owner: holder of `d_karaite_exilarchate` (Boaz I of the Ananites at 867).
- **The Mourners of Zion**: Daniel al-Qumisi (9th century) calls Karaites to Jerusalem; the Nasi can
  move the community's centre there: ascetics, mourning for Zion, the Karaite golden age of Jerusalem
- **Polemics**: Saadia Gaon's attacks and the Karaite replies (Salmon ben Jeroham), as debate events with
  the Rabbinic Exilarch and the Geonim
- **The calendar**: sighting the new moon and the ripening barley (*aviv*) in the Land, a yearly event that
  ties the Karaites to Jerusalem
- **Missions**: Karaite scholars and merchants to Byzantium and Egypt
- **The end in Jerusalem**: the crusader conquest of 1099 (Karaite books and captives ransomed); later
  centres in Egypt and Constantinople

### `story_jip_beit_nathan`: the lost crown
Owner: head of Beit Nathan (`nathanite_dynasty`; Pinchas ben Abdimi's Mishpacha family at 867), the house of
the Exilarchs before Bostanai.
- **The old claim**: Beit Nathan descends from the Exilarchs of the Talmud and Persian era (Huna, Zutra,
  Kahana); the story keeps the claim alive with genealogies, elders and Geonim who remember
- **The Bostanai controversy**: Bostanai's sons by his Jewish wife called the sons of the Persian princess
  slaves, and their legitimacy was disputed for centuries. Beit Nathan can use it against whichever
  Bostanite holds the title
- **The road back**: the vanilla Claim Exilarchate decision and scheme (already in the mod), alliances with
  a Gaon in a quarrel (the Rabbinic story's crises are openings), courting electors
- **Another crown**: if the Exilarchate stays out of reach, found a Davidic *nesiut* elsewhere, as Davidic
  families later did in Mosul, Damascus and Egypt
- **Ending**: Beit Nathan regains `d_exilarchate`, founds its own nesiut, or the line dies out

### The feud of Beit Bustenai and Beit Anan
The two Exilarchic houses come from one family. In the Rabbanite telling, Anan ben David was passed over
for the Exilarchate in favour of his younger brother Hananiah (c. 760s), was imprisoned by the caliph, and
founded the sect that became the Karaites. The Bostanite and Ananite lines have been rivals ever since.

- [ ] **A vanilla house relation at feud level** between the Bostanite house and the Ananite house, set at
  game start with `set_house_relation` (`level = feud`, via vanilla's `set_house_relation_level_effect`),
  wherever both houses exist. Both heads start as playable Abbasid vassals, so vanilla's
  `default_house_relation` accepts it (shared top liege)
- [ ] **What the feud level gives** (vanilla): -30 opinion between members, hostile scheme bonus, eradicate
  casus belli, cheaper casus belli prestige, fewer marriages, fewer guests at each other's activities, more
  likely war targets
- [ ] **Reason text**: a description key like `house_relation_reason_jip_anan_passed_over_desc` ("Anan was
  passed over for his brother")
- [ ] **Moving the relation**: the story cycles shift it with `change_house_relation_level`
  - deeper: polemics between the two Exilarchs (the Karaite story's Saadia events), a rival Exilarch raised
    by either side, a slight at a feast
  - easing: a marriage between the houses, standing together against a caliph's fines or a persecution, a
    shared Pesach (section 1)
  - full reconciliation only as a rare, hard-won outcome; the schism stays
- [ ] **Starting heads at 867**: Hasdai II (Bostanite, Rabbinic Exilarch) and Boaz I (Ananite, Karaite Nasi);
  check the two houses' keys in the mod's dynasty files

### Reconciliation: one Israel again
The way out of the feud: Rabbanites and Karaites rejoin, either as rites of one faith or by the smaller
community merging into the dominant one. History never got that far, but it came close to coexistence: in
Fatimid Egypt the two communities cooperated, intermarried with marriage contracts that honoured both
customs, and shared leaders (Rustow). That's the ground this path builds on.

**Where things stand in the mod**
- `rabbinic_faith` has the rites `rabbinism` and `merkabah`; `karaism` is its own faith with one rite,
  `karaism` (`common/religion/rite_types/jip_jewish_rite_types.txt`), both in `judaism_religion`
- **Engine effects** (from the docs in `ck3.exe`):
  - `set_parent_faith = { target = <faith> main = no include_derived = yes }` on a rite moves it into
    another faith
  - `set_character_rite` and `set_county_rite` convert characters and counties to a rite
  - `detach_rite_to_new_faith` splits a rite off into a new faith (for a renewed schism)
  - `set_can_convert_to_rite` opens or closes a rite to conversion

**The road there**
- [ ] **Easing the feud first**: the house relation must have climbed out of feud (cordial or better) through
  the story cycles' events: marriages, joint stands against the caliph, shared feasts
- [ ] **Mixed marriages**: a marriage between members of the two communities can carry a double contract
  (both customs honoured), easing the relation and making the next step likelier (the Geniza contracts)
- [ ] **The assembly**: a decision for either Exilarch, once relations allow, to call the other, the Geonim
  and the Karaite scholars to an assembly. An event chain of debate (learning, diplomacy, piety, the
  attendees' opinions and zeal) decides the outcome. AI Exilarchs need high opinion and low zeal to agree

**Outcomes**
1. **Union of rites** (the two are close in strength, or the assembly agrees on equal standing)
   - the smaller faith's rite moves into the larger with `set_parent_faith` (usually Karaite into
     `rabbinic_faith`, giving it Rabbinic, Merkabah and Karaite rites); if Karaism is the larger, the
     Rabbinic rites move instead
   - characters and counties keep their rite and now share one faith: no faith hostility between them
   - both titles survive: `d_karaite_exilarchate` becomes the Karaite rite's Nasi alongside the Exilarch
   - test in game: what the emptied faith does, and whether the engine swaps the moved rite's tenets or
     doctrines (its docs mention divergence-driven swaps when rites change)
2. **Merge into the dominant faith** (one side clearly stronger: e.g. far more counties and followers, the
   holy sites, or it won the assembly or a war under the feud's eradicate casus belli)
   - the minority converts to the dominant faith's main rite: counties over time (a fast conversion
     modifier, then `set_county_rite`), characters by choice, with zealots refusing (they keep the old faith,
     or hold it in secret)
   - the losing line's Exilarch title becomes ceremonial or is destroyed
3. **Failure**: the assembly breaks up; the relation drops back towards feud and a cooldown starts

**Afterwards**
- [ ] **A renewed schism**: in a union, if the houses fall back into feud and zeal runs high, the Karaite (or
  Rabbinic) rite can split off again through `detach_rite_to_new_faith`
- [ ] **Measuring dominance**: a script value comparing the two faiths' counties, followers and holy sites,
  shown in the decision's tooltip so the player knows which outcome is in reach
- [ ] **Who it's for**: available only while both houses (and both Exilarchates) exist; the Hasmonean and
  Beit Nathan stories react to it (a reunited Israel raises the Hasmonean return's prestige)

**Reading**: Marina Rustow, *Heresy and the Politics of Community: The Jews of the Fatimid Caliphate*
(Cornell, 2008)

## 5. Eldad ha-Dani (adventurer)

The 9th-century traveller who claimed to come from the tribe of Dan, of a Jewish kingdom of four tribes
beyond the river Sambation, and turned up at Kairouan around 883. His account and his unusual laws
reached Tzemah Gaon of Sura as a question.

- [ ] **Vanilla landless adventurer** (`landless_adventurer_government`), **not Mishpacha**
- [ ] **Title** `d_laamp_eldad` in `common/landed_titles/jip_landless_titles.txt`, built like vanilla's
  `d_laamp_el_cid` (`landless`, `require_landless`, `dlc_feature = roads_to_power`), with its holder in
  `history/titles/` and the character in `history/characters/`
- [ ] **Start**: playable at 867, in East Africa or Arabia before his travels; birth date unknown, pick a
  plausible one (alive and travelling in the 880s)
- [ ] **Faith and culture** (decided): `faith = haymanot`, `culture = ethiopian` (vanilla). His laws differed
  from the Rabbanites', and the Beta Israel later traced their descent from Dan through him
- [ ] **Flavour**
  - his tales: shipwreck, captivity among cannibals, ransom by a Jewish merchant of Issachar
  - telling them at courts for prestige; vanilla legends for "the kingdom beyond the Sambation"
  - the Kairouan question and Tzemah Gaon's reply from Sura: a judgment on his laws (piety or doubt)

## 6. Kohanism waits for the Temple

Kohanism is the faith of the Temple priesthood, so no one can convert to it while there is no Temple.

**Where things stand**: Kohanism is its own faith with one rite, `kohanism`
(`common/religion/rite_types/jip_jewish_rite_types.txt`), open to conversion today. The Temple is rebuilt by
the Clear the Grounds for the Third Temple decision, whose `rebuild_the_third_temple_effect`
(`common/scripted_effects/01_jip_decisions_effects.txt`) places `temple_in_jerusalem_01` in Jerusalem.

- [ ] **Closed at the start**: `convert = no` on the `kohanism` rite (a rite-type field: characters can't
  convert to it)
- [ ] **Opened by the Temple**: `rebuild_the_third_temple_effect` runs
  `rite:kohanism ?= { set_can_convert_to_rite = yes }`, with a line in the decision's effects saying
  Kohanism is now open
- [ ] **Closed again if the Temple falls**: a hidden yearly check (an event on a global yearly on_action's
  event list, since on_action effects can't be appended to) closes conversion again whenever
  `c_jerusalem`'s province lacks `temple_in_jerusalem_01`, and opens it when the building is there. This
  also keeps old saves right. Existing Kohanists keep their faith
- [ ] **Test in game**:
  - whether `convert = no` also stops counties converting (court chaplains, the convert-county
    interaction) or only characters; if counties still can, block them in the same way
  - that children of Kohanists are still born Kohanist (birth isn't conversion)
- [ ] **Interactions**: the Hasmonean priest-kingship (section 3) and the Korban Pesach (section 1) already
  wait for the Temple, so they line up with Kohanism opening

## 7. The Temple as a great project

Today the Temple is one decision, `clear_grounds_for_the_third_temple_decision`
(`common/decisions/jip_jewish_decisions.txt`): an independent Jewish ruler of Israelite heritage who holds
Jerusalem and completely controls `k_israel`, at piety level 4, pays 500 piety and fires `jip_hebrew.0041`,
whose `rebuild_the_third_temple_effect` removes the Dome of the Rock, places `temple_in_jerusalem_01`, takes
Jerusalem from the Islamic faiths' holy sites and costs them 15 fervour. Turn this into vanilla great
projects, so the Temple is built over years with others' help, and its cost is felt while it rises.

### How vanilla does it (the model)
- Great projects live in `common/great_projects/types/` (documented in `_great_project_types.info`):
  `is_shown`, `can_start_planning`, `is_location_valid`, `province_filter`, `owner`, `cost`,
  `construction_time`, `is_valid`, `project_contributions` (each with its own cost, `is_required`, and
  `on_contribution_funded`/`on_complete`), and `on_plan_build`, `on_start_build`, `on_complete`,
  `on_cancel`, `on_invalidated`
- The closest model is the cathedral, `pam_cathedral_01`–`03` (`01_pam_projects.txt`): tiers, required and
  optional contributions, a special building on completion
- **Who can contribute** is a fixed set (`allowed_contributor_filter`): vassals, tributaries, liege, top
  liege, owner, allies, head of faith, founder, duchy, clerical region, puppeteer, councillors. There is no
  "same faith" option
- **DLC**: the cathedral is gated by `has_pam_dlc_trigger` and the Chinese projects by
  `has_tgp_dlc_trigger`. Check whether the great-project interface works without either; if not, keep the
  decision as the fallback for players without them

### The projects
- [ ] **Stage 1, `jip_gp_clear_the_temple_mount`**: clearing the Mount
  - founder: the decision's current requirements (independent, Jewish, Israelite heritage, holds Jerusalem,
    controls `k_israel`, piety level 4); location: Jerusalem's province only
  - `on_start_build`: the Muslim world learns of it; Muslim rulers' opinion falls and holy wars against the
    founder become likelier for as long as it takes
  - `on_complete`: the first half of today's effect: the Dome of the Rock removed, Jerusalem taken from the
    Islamic faiths' holy sites, the fervour loss, the -100 opinion
  - `is_valid`: the founder's realm still holds Jerusalem and stays Jewish
  - this is the most provocative act in the mod: keep its text sober
- [ ] **Stage 2, `jip_gp_rebuild_the_temple`**: building the Temple, open once stage 1 is complete
  - required contributions:
    - **dressed stone** from the founder's realm
    - **cedars of Lebanon** from the holder of the Lebanese coast (Tyre or Sidon), as Hiram of Tyre sent them
      to Solomon: an ally or vassal, or bought at a high price
  - optional contributions:
    - **the priestly vestments**, from the Kohen Gadol (head of faith)
    - **gold and the vessels**, the menorah and the altar
    - **the Levites' song**
  - `on_complete`: the rest of today's effect (`temple_in_jerusalem_01`) plus opening Kohanism to conversion
    (section 6); `jip_hebrew.0041` becomes the completion event
- [ ] **The half-shekel**: the diaspora's Temple tax, outside the contributor filter. A decision or
  interaction lets any Jewish ruler or Mishpacha head send gold to the project's founder (each sender gains
  piety and the founder's opinion; the project's progress or a cost discount grows with the total)
- [ ] **Retire the decision**: `clear_grounds_for_the_third_temple_decision` gives way to stage 1 (or stays
  only as the no-DLC fallback); `rebuild_the_third_temple_effect` splits into the two stages' effects
- [ ] **AI**: rarely, and only rulers with the zeal for it; never more than one Temple project at a time

## 8. The Palestinian Gaonate

The yeshiva of the Land of Israel, at Tiberias and from the early 10th century in Jerusalem (after 1071 Tyre,
then Damascus). Its geonim came from a few priestly families (Ben Meir; the Kohen family of Evyatar
ha-Kohen), and once from a Davidic one: Daniel ben Azarya, from Babylon, held it 1051–1062.

- [ ] **Title** `d_palestinian_gaonate`, landless and built like `d_exilarchate`; seat Tiberias (`c_tiberias`)
  at 867, moving to Jerusalem by an event in the early 10th century
- [ ] **Holder at 867**: a new Mishpacha family (the Ben Meir house); succession elective among the yeshiva's
  priestly families
- [ ] **The Mount of Olives assembly**: on Hoshana Rabba, the end of Sukkot (section 2), a Gaon who holds
  Sukkot in Jerusalem holds the assembly: proclaims the year's calendar, gathers pilgrims and donations,
  and may ban the Karaites (Karaite opinion falls; the Bostanai–Anan feud, section 4, deepens)
- [ ] **Against Babylon**: the Ben Meir calendar dispute (921–922), when the Palestinian Gaon proclaimed a
  calendar Saadia and the Babylonian academies rejected: a crisis in the Rabbinic Exilarch story where the
  Exilarch must choose a side, and the losing side loses standing
- [ ] **A Davidic Gaon**: after Daniel ben Azarya, the Rabbinic and Beit Nathan stories can put a Davidic
  candidate forward

## 9. The Nagid of Egypt

The head of the Jews (*ra'is al-yahud*) of Fatimid Egypt, appointed by the caliph over Rabbanites, Karaites
and Samaritans alike. The office appears around 1065 (Judah ibn Saadia, then his brother Mevorakh); from
1082 to 1094 David ben Daniel, Daniel ben Azarya's Davidic son, held it.

- [ ] **Title** `d_negidut_egypt`, landless, seat `c_cairo`
